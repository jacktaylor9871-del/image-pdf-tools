How it works

1

Add your PDF

Choose the file from your device. You will see its size and page count before you start.

2

Pick a level

Maximum compression suits strict upload limits (like 100 KB or 200 KB). Recommended keeps documents clearly readable.

3

Download

Each page is re-rendered as an optimized image and rebuilt into a new, smaller PDF you can upload right away.

Worth knowing: because pages are rebuilt as images, text in the compressed PDF is no longer selectable or searchable, and signatures/stamps become part of the page image. That is fine for form uploads and printing.

If your PDF is mostly typed text with few images, it may already be small — in that case the compressed copy can occasionally come out larger, and the tool will tell you instead of pretending otherwise.

Common questions

What size should I choose for online forms and uploads?

Most US job portals, college application systems, and government upload pages (such as USCIS and IRS document uploads) accept roughly 200 KB to 2 MB per file, and Gmail caps attachments at 25 MB. Start with Recommended; if the result is still above the limit, run it again on Maximum, and switch Color to Grayscale if the document is a plain scan — that usually cuts the size a lot more.

How do I compress a PDF to 100 KB or 200 KB?

Choose Maximum compression, set Color to Grayscale if the document is a black-and-white scan, and lower the Image quality slider toward 30–40%. Compress once, check the result size, and run it again if you still need it smaller. Very long documents may not reach 100 KB without losing readability — in that case, split the PDF and upload it in parts if the portal allows it.

Why did my PDF not get much smaller?

PDFs made of plain typed text are already compact, so there is little left to squeeze. The biggest savings come from scanned documents, photos, and PDFs exported from phone scanner apps, which often carry very large images.

Will the quality be good enough to read and print?

At Recommended (150 DPI) and Best quality (200 DPI), text stays clear on screen and on US Letter prints. Maximum (96 DPI) is meant for upload limits, not printing — small text can look soft.

My PDF has a password. Can it be compressed?

No — password-protected (encrypted) PDFs cannot be opened by this tool. Remove the password first using the app you created it with, then compress it here.
